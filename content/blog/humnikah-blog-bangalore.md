## What a Muslim marriage bureau actually does

A marriage bureau is a human go-between. Instead of searching thousands of profiles alone, you share your biodata and expectations with a matchmaker, who looks for families that fit and arranges introductions.

In practice, a good bureau will:

- collect and review biodata from both sides
- understand your family's expectations on faith, education, work, language and community
- check basic details before introducing anyone
- introduce families respectfully, with the Wali involved where the bride's family wants this
- coordinate meetings and keep your details private
- stay in touch until families decide

A bureau cannot guarantee a marriage, choose a spouse for you, or replace your own istikhara, enquiries and consultation with elders. Its job is to make the search safer, more private and less tiring.

## 10 questions to ask before you choose a bureau

Use these with any Muslim marriage bureau in Bangalore, including us. A good bureau will answer clearly and will not be offended.

1. **How do you screen profiles before introducing families?** You want a real process, not "we trust everyone".
2. **What do you verify, and how?** Typical checks are identity, address, education and work. Ask what is checked in person and what is only taken from documents.
3. **What do you not verify?** An honest bureau will tell you its limits.
4. **Will the Wali and the family be involved at each step?** Introductions should go through families, not around them.
5. **How is my biodata shared, and with whom?** Your details should not be forwarded to WhatsApp groups or posted publicly without your consent.
6. **What are your fees, when are they due, and will I get a receipt?** Get this in writing before you hand over money or documents.
7. **Do you promise a match or a timeline?** No honest bureau can. Be careful with "100% matches" and "marriage in 30 days".
8. **Can you work with my language, community and family expectations?** In Bangalore, families often look for Urdu-, Kannada-, Beary- or Bhatkali-speaking matches, and the bureau should listen without pushing you.
9. **Who will be my point of contact, and how quickly do you reply?** You should know the name of the person handling your file.
10. **Can I visit the office and meet the team?** A physical address and a real person to speak to are good signs.

## How verification works, and what it cannot confirm

Verification means checking that the basic facts in a biodata are true. It usually covers:

- **Identity:** a government ID such as Aadhaar or PAN
- **Address:** proof of address, and sometimes a visit to the home
- **Education and work:** certificates, an offer letter or a payslip
- **Family details:** confirming who the family members are

Verification helps remove fake and careless profiles. But it has limits. A document shows what a person has, not what their character is like. No bureau can read a person's heart or predict a marriage.

## Red flags to watch for

Walk away, or at least slow down, if a bureau:

- guarantees a match, or says every profile is "100% genuine"
- asks you to pay the full fee before it has explained its process or shown how it will help
- gives no written terms and no receipt
- cannot or will not explain how it verifies profiles
- asks for sensitive details such as bank passwords, OTPs or card numbers (a bureau never needs these)
- shares your biodata or photographs widely without your permission
- pressures you to decide quickly or hides the other family's details until you pay
- ignores your family's wishes or tries to bypass the Wali
